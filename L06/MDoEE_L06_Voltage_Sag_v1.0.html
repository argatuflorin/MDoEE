01. What is a voltage sag?

A temporary reduction in the root-mean-square (RMS) voltage below a detection threshold, followed by recovery. The fundamental frequency can remain at 50 Hz: it is not necessarily the frequency that decreases, but the voltage amplitude.

For this lesson's exercises, we use a starting threshold of 90% of Un and an ending threshold of 92% of Un (2% hysteresis), as described in the supplied material. Interruption thresholds and conventions are specified separately.

u(t) = √2 · URMS(t) · sin(2πft + φ)
Important distinction: the instantaneous waveform crosses zero every half-cycle, even under normal operation. This does not mean an interruption. The event is detected using RMS voltage, not sinusoidal zero crossings.

Event parameters

Ures = min URMS
d = (Un − Ures)/Un · 100%
Δt = tend − tstart

Example: Un = 230 V, Ures = 115 V → residual voltage 50%, sag depth 50%.

Why might an installation remain stopped after voltage recovery?

A self-holding contactor may drop out and require a new START command. A PLC may restart or remain stopped depending on its supply and configuration. A motor may require protective devices to be reset. Process recovery does not necessarily coincide with voltage recovery.

Approximate torque–voltage relationship

For an induction motor under comparable conditions and constant frequency, electromagnetic torque is approximately proportional to the square of the voltage:

M / Mn ≈ (U / Un)²

At 70% of nominal voltage → approximately 49% of the available torque under the same conditions. This is an illustrative model; actual dynamics depend on load, slip, and sag duration.

Auto-reclosing, automatic transfer, UPS, and static transfer — what does each do?

RAR

Fast automatic reclosing: after tripping, the circuit breaker attempts to reclose following a transient fault.

Networks / feeders

AAR

Automatic transfer to standby supply: the load is transferred to an available source after isolating the normal supply and verifying the required conditions.

Power supply continuity

UPS

Stores energy and supplies critical loads during a voltage sag, subject to autonomy and topology constraints.

Rapidly selects between suitable sources, with strict synchronization and compatibility requirements.

Numerical classroom exercise

An analyzer records an event with a nominal voltage of 230 V, a minimum voltage of 92 V, and a duration of 250 ms.

Tasks: calculate residual voltage as a percentage, sag depth as a percentage, duration in 50 Hz cycles, and explain whether the frequency must necessarily change.

Show solution

Ures/Un = 92/230 = 40%. Sag depth = 60%. T = 1/50 = 20 ms. Number of cycles = 250/20 = 12.5. Frequency may remain at 50 Hz.

For the physical laboratory

Use a PQ analyzer capable of recording RMS variations at appropriate resolution, affected phases, timestamps, and events. Do not create short circuits in a 230/400 V installation for demonstration; use an isolated, controlled laboratory emulator or source.
